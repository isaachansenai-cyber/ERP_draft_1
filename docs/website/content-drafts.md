## Our Countries page

### Brazil: Chicken Strogonoff
A dish that traveled the world before settling in Brazil. Stroganoff began in 19th-century Russia. As it moved through Europe and across the Atlantic, Brazilian cooks made it their own: tender chicken in a creamy sauce brightened with a little tomato and mustard, served with white rice and crunchy *batata palha* (shoestring potatoes). In Brazil it is a family favorite, the kind of dish that means a comfortable Sunday at home.
*Ready in about 25 minutes.*

### Mexico: Tacos al Pastor
Al pastor is a story of migration. Lebanese families who settled in Mexico in the early 20th century brought spit-roasted meat with them. Over the decades Mexican cooks transformed it with pork, dried chiles, achiote and pineapple, and it became a taco icon of Mexico City. Our version marinates pork overnight in guajillo chiles, achiote and spices, then serves it on corn tortillas with pineapple, onion, cilantro and lime.
*Prepared a day ahead: order by the day before.*

### Korea: Korean Fried Chicken with Rice
Fried chicken arrived in Korea in the mid-20th century, and Korean cooks made it unmistakably their own. Double-coated for an audible crunch, then glazed in a sweet-and-spicy sauce built on **gochujang**, a fermented chili paste at the heart of Korean cooking. In Korea, fried chicken is something friends share. We finish ours with sesame seeds and scallions, and serve it with rice.
*Ready in about 20 minutes.*

### France: Beef Bourguignon with Potatoes
From Burgundy in eastern France, bourguignon is farmhouse cooking at its most patient: beef braised slowly with mushrooms, carrots, onion and thyme until it falls apart. The classic uses Burgundy wine. **Ours is alcohol-free:** we braise with unsweetened red grape juice, so everyone can enjoy it.
*Braised a day ahead: order by the day before.*

### Mongolia: Khorkhog
Khorkhog is a celebration dish from Mongolia's open grasslands. Traditionally, meat and vegetables are cooked in a sealed container with stones heated in a fire. The hot stones steam and roast everything from the inside, and guests often pass the warm stones from hand to hand. Traditional khorkhog is usually mutton or goat. Ours uses lamb shoulder with potatoes, carrots, onion and garlic, seasoned simply with salt and pepper so the meat speaks for itself.
*Cooked fresh the same day: about 2 hours.*

## FAQ

**Do you deliver?**
Not at the moment. Every order is for pickup at the location you choose.

**What are your hours?**
12:00 to 8:00 pm, every day, at Provo, Spanish Fork and Salt Lake City. The last pickup time is 7:45 pm.

**Why can't I get some dishes today?**
Our Tacos al Pastor marinate overnight and our Beef Bourguignon braises the day before, so they're ready from the next day. Everything else is made fresh the same day.

**How far ahead can I order?**
Up to 7 days ahead.

**How do I pay?**
[Demo] Checkout uses a simulated payment. No real card is charged.
[Pilot] You pay at the counter when you pick up.

**Can I change or cancel my order online?**
Not online. Please call the location you ordered from, and we'll help.

**What if a dish shows "Sold out"?**
We only accept orders we can actually make. A dish comes back as soon as we restock, usually without you having to refresh.

**Do you have allergen information?**
Every dish lists dairy, wheat and nut information. If you have a serious allergy, please call the location before you order. Our kitchen handles many ingredients, so we can't guarantee that any dish is allergen-free.

**Is anything cooked with alcohol?**
No. Our Beef Bourguignon uses red grape juice instead of wine.

**I didn't pick up my order. What happens?**
Orders not collected by closing are cancelled at the end of the day.
